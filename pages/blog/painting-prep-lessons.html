Painting Prep Lessons From Real Jobs

Painting looks like the easy trade. The prep is where jobs go right or wrong.

Painting Prep Lessons From Real Jobs

Quick answer: Most painting problems start before the first coat. Check the surface, especially unprimed or imperfect drywall, patch and prime before you paint, and plan supplies so you only make one trip to the store. Keep a touch-up painting kit in your tool storage so a patch never stops a job.

We thought painting would be a good fit for us because it does not have a steep learning curve. It does not, but it has nuances, and a few of them cost us time and return trips before we learned them. Here is what we do differently now.

1. Look at the drywall before you open the paint

Unprimed drywall and walls with lots of imperfections are the biggest surprises. Bare drywall soaks up paint unevenly, and imperfections show through a finish coat. Walk the walls with a light, mark every flaw, patch and sand, and prime before the first coat goes on. That takes more time up front and saves the return trips later. See our drywall repair guide.

2. Make one trip to the store

We used to show up, patch a wall, and then realize we had no paint supplies, so someone drove to the store. Now every painting job starts with a standard checklist, ordered at the same time as the paint: masking materials, tape, drop cloths, rollers, brushes, and a few extras. One trip, then work. You may end up with extra brushes, but you will use them, and having them on hand is far better than a store run in the middle of a job.

3. Carry a touch-up kit everywhere

We now keep basic painting supplies in each of our tool storage sets, so that wherever a job takes you, you can do touch-up painting. A single dedicated tote, a painting bucket with everything you need, means there is no sifting through your tools to find the right one.

4. Tape and protect first

Careful masking is the difference between a clean line and a weekend of scraping. Take the time to tape windows, trim, and floors, as in the photo above, before you start. Our room painting guide goes through the full order.

5. Maintain a sprayer, or skip it

A paint sprayer is excellent for fences and large interior surfaces, but poor maintenance can force you to replace it mid-job. If you use one, clean it fully every time.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to prime drywall before painting?

New or freshly patched drywall should be primed so the paint absorbs evenly. Imperfections should be patched and sanded first.

What painting supplies should I buy with the paint?

Masking tape and materials, drop cloths, rollers, and brushes. Buying them in the same trip saves time.

Is a paint sprayer worth it?

It can save a lot of time on big or repeated jobs, including fences, but it needs proper cleaning and maintenance.
